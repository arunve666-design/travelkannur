About Theyyam

What is Theyyam?

Theyyam is a ritualistic performance art that is over 1,000 years old, practised primarily in Kannur and Kasaragod districts of Kerala. The word Theyyam means "God" in the local dialect — during the performance, the performer is believed to literally become the deity.

Performers wear elaborate costumes up to 30 feet tall, striking face paint, and dance in a trance-like state to drumming and chanting. Watching a Theyyam is one of the most powerful cultural experiences available anywhere in the world — and it's completely free to attend.

Visitor Guide

How to Experience Theyyam

🎟️

Entry is Free

All Theyyam performances are free to attend. They are religious festivals open to everyone regardless of religion or nationality.

🌙

Go at Night

Most major Theyyams begin after dark and last until dawn. The atmosphere by torchlight is absolutely otherworldly.

👗

Dress Respectfully

These are religious ceremonies. Dress modestly — no shorts or sleeveless tops. Remove shoes before entering temple grounds.

📸

Photography

Generally allowed but ask locals first. Never use flash during the performance. Keep a respectful distance from the performer.

🚗

Getting There

Hire a local taxi or auto-rickshaw. Ask your hotel to arrange transport — most drivers know the festival locations well.

🌟

Best for First Timers

Parassinikkadavu Muthappan Temple has daily Theyyam year-round — the easiest and most accessible for first-time visitors.
